# Example 3: Cold outreach email

Invented sender: an independent bookkeeper offering a free resource to a small-business blog. The "before" email is made up to show common mistakes.

## Before

> **Subject:** Partnership Opportunity!!
>
> Hi there,
>
> My name is Sam and I run a bookkeeping firm with over 10 years of experience. We offer a wide range of services including bookkeeping, payroll and tax preparation. I'd love to jump on a 30-minute call to discuss how we could collaborate and bring value to your audience.
>
> Let me know when you're free!
> Sam

## After

> **Subject:** Two numbers in your "first hire" guide
>
> Hi Priya,
>
> Your guide to hiring a first employee is the clearest I've read, especially the checklist at the end.
>
> Two figures in the payroll-cost section look out of date since this year's rate change [confirm]. I've put the corrected numbers and the official source in three lines below, so you can paste them in as they are.
>
> For context: I do the books for about 60 small businesses [confirm], so these rates are what I file every month.
>
> Would a one-page "true cost of a first hire" calculator be useful for your readers? Happy to send it, no strings.
>
> Sam Ortiz, bookkeeper

## What changed and why

- Subject line is specific to her page and under 50 characters; no exclamation marks or "opportunity". [SB] [LEADS]
- First lines are about her work, with a concrete compliment, not about the sender. [LEADS] [GW]
- Leads with big, fast value (corrected numbers ready to paste) instead of asking for a call. [LEADS]
- Credibility in one clause ("for context"), with a specific number. [OW]
- Ends with one small, easy yes; a free lead magnet rather than a pitch. [LEADS] [OFF]
