# Topic research: finding the 50 easy topics

How to run the competition test from `../SKILL.md`. Sources are tagged; untagged rules are this
skill's working defaults.

## 1. Seeds
- Write 20–30 seed phrases from the reader's problems and situations: "how to", "best", "vs",
  "cost of", "can you", "why does", "near", "for beginners", "with kids", "in winter".
- Add the reader's own words: read forum threads, app reviews, product reviews and social comments
  and copy the phrasing people actually use.

## 2. Expand
- **Keyword tool.** Keysearch, Ahrefs, Semrush, LowFruits and similar tools give monthly searches, a
  difficulty score on a 0–100 scale and related terms. Different tools disagree; stick to one tool
  per comparison and note which. Keysearch, for example, lets you save terms to lists and re-check
  them later, which suits a 50-topic test.
- **Autocomplete, "alphabet soup".** Type the seed into Google followed by each letter (a, b, c...)
  or by a question word, and harvest the suggestions. Income School popularised this as a way to
  find long questions keyword tools miss. [IS]
- **Results-page extras.** "People also ask" boxes, related searches at the bottom of the page,
  and the subheadings of the pages that rank.
- **Question mining.** Reddit, niche forums, Quora, Facebook groups, YouTube comments and app store
  reviews show questions people ask that nobody has written a page for. Questions that keep
  repeating are topics.

## 3. Filter on numbers (shortlist only)
- Starting bars: 50+ monthly searches for a single term, or a cluster of close variants (same
  answer, different wording) adding up to 100+; difficulty 30 or less on a 0–100 scale.
- "-" or blank volume means the tool has no data, not zero. Keep such terms in a separate
  "no data" group; some are real long-tail questions worth a post.
- Note the tool, the country or language setting and the date beside every number.

## 4. Read the results page (the verdict)
Anne Moss's point: tool scores can be badly misleading, so the results page decides. [AM]
Signs the topic is easy:
- Forum threads, Q&A pages or social posts ranking in the top 10. [IS]
- Pages that answer a different or broader question, or mention the topic in passing.
- Old, thin, or clearly outdated pages; pages from small sites.
- Few or no pages with the exact phrase in the title.
Signs it is hard:
- Big brands, official sources and large publishers filling the first page.
- Several recent, thorough pages that answer exactly this question.
- A results page dominated by video, shopping or map results that a blog post cannot replace.

## 5. A quick ratio check (optional)
**Keyword Golden Ratio** [KGR]: run an `allintitle:"phrase"` search, divide the number of results
by the monthly searches. For terms under about 250 monthly searches, a ratio under 0.25 suggests few
pages target the phrase, so a new site can rank quickly; 0.25–1 is possible; above 1 is crowded.
It is a rough filter, not a verdict.

## 6. Count and record
- Time-box it: 2–3 hours. 50 good topics = go on; 10–15 after two hours = skip. [AM]
- Record one row per topic: topic, best term, monthly searches, difficulty, tool and date, what
  ranks now (one line), the post you would write, and "not checked" where you have no data.
- Group variants that one post can answer; count the post once.

## 7. Before you commit
- Look at the 12-month trend for the top terms to catch seasonality or a fading fad.
- Check whether the questions keep coming (new products, rules, places, seasons) or run out.
- Re-check a sample of the list a month later if the decision can wait; scores drift.
