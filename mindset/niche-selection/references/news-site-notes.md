# News and curation sites

For ideas that publish daily (news, alerts, openings and closings, rule changes, events, deals)
rather than evergreen guides. Sources are tagged; untagged rules are this skill's working
defaults. This is not legal advice: check the law of the country you publish from and about.

## 1. Daily sources
A news niche is only as good as its steady, lawful supply of stories. List at least five, and
prefer primary sources over other publishers:
- Official press releases and newsrooms (companies, venues, transport operators, tourism boards).
- Government and regulator notices, statistics releases and open data portals.
- Event, venue and ticketing listings; trade fairs; release calendars.
- Local-language press and broadcasters (as leads to follow up, not text to copy).
- Community reports, readers' tips, your own visits, photos and data.
- Public records where publishing them is lawful and fair.
Note for each: how often it updates, how you collect it (RSS, email alert, API, a page check), the
language, and whether its terms allow re-use.

## 2. Originality over rewriting [GS]
- Google's guidance asks whether content is made for people (useful, original, from first-hand
  knowledge or real effort) or made mainly to attract search visits. Its questions include whether
  the page adds substantial value beyond what others already published, and whether it only
  summarises other sources without adding anything.
- Google's spam policies (expanded in March 2024) define "scaled content abuse": producing many
  pages mainly to manipulate rankings that add little value for readers, however they are made
  (by hand, by script or with automated tools), including stitching or rewriting other sites'
  content and feeds.
- What counts as added value on a news site: a local-language source made understandable in another
  language (summary in your own words, link to the original), context and background, "what this
  means for you" (dates, costs, who is affected, what to do), your own photos or reporting, data you
  collect over time (trackers, calendars, price histories, maps), and fast, accurate corrections.

## 3. Site reputation abuse ("parasite" posts) [GS]
- Selling or giving space on a trusted site to third-party pages that exist to borrow its ranking
  power (casino, payday loans, coupons, unrelated reviews) is "site reputation abuse" under Google's
  spam policies (announced March 2024, enforced from May 2024, clarified in November 2024 to cover
  such content even when the host is involved in producing it).
- Penalties can hit the section or the whole site. Sponsored posts that fit the audience, are
  labelled and use marked (sponsored or nofollow) links are a different thing; keep them rare.

## 4. Legal checks before launch
- **Copyright.** Facts and news events themselves are generally not protected; the article written
  about them is. A full translation of someone's article is a version of their work and needs
  permission. Write your own summary from the facts, quote only short passages that you need, with
  the source named and linked. Quotation rules differ by country (some allow quotation only when it
  is clearly secondary to your own text and properly credited); check the local rule. Photos and
  video need their own permission or licence.
- **Defamation.** Report arrests, accusations and lawsuits as what they are, from an official or
  named source, with the date. Do not add guesses, nicknames or loaded words. Think hard before
  naming private people. Some countries treat defamation as a crime and do not accept "it was true"
  as enough on its own (a public-interest purpose may also be required). Have a correction and
  takedown process and use it fast.
- **Privacy and platform rules.** Victims, minors and bystanders deserve extra care; ad networks
  and social platforms add their own rules on violent or sexual content.
- **Trademarks.** Keep other companies' brand names out of the domain.
- When the model depends on crime, celebrity or tabloid stories, get a lawyer's view first.

## 5. The automated pipeline, with a human in it
A safe default shape:
1. **Collect:** pull new items from the source list on a schedule (feeds, alerts, page checks).
2. **De-duplicate and filter:** drop repeats and off-topic items; score relevance to the reader.
3. **Draft:** a short summary in your own words with the key facts (what, when, where, cost, who is
   affected), the source named and linked, and a suggested headline.
4. **Human review (required):** a person approves, edits or skips each story; checks names,
   numbers, dates and claims against the original; and decides the angle. Crime, health, money,
   legal and named-private-person stories never publish without this step.
5. **Publish:** with a byline, the date, the source link and the right category; update older
   stories instead of duplicating them.
6. **Share:** newsletter, social posts and links from the owner's related sites where relevant.
7. **Measure and correct:** log every published item, its source and reviewer; act on corrections
   within a day; review traffic, revenue per post and complaints monthly.
Keep a daily review budget (for example 20–30 minutes) and publish fewer, better stories when it
runs out.

## 6. Trust signals [QRG] [GS]
- Named authors with a short bio and relevant experience.
- An about page (who runs the site, who owns it, how it is funded), a contact route, an editorial
  and corrections policy, and clear labels for sponsored content and affiliate links.
- Dates on every story and visible updates when facts change.

## 7. Money on news sites
- Display ads pay per view, so news needs volume; ad-restricted subjects (crime, violence, sexual
  content, gambling) earn less per view under ad network policies and advertiser filters. [PUB]
- Other routes: a newsletter with sponsors, affiliate links on practical stories (tickets, tours,
  apps), reader support or memberships, leads for the owner's own businesses, licensing data.
