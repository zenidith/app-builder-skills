# Example 1: comparing three evergreen niches

Invented candidates and invented numbers, to show the method. Not research on real markets.

## Candidates
- **A. Dwarf shrimp keeping** — small-aquarium owners who keep losing shrimp; return for each new
  species and problem. Microniche (about 400–600 posts).
- **B. Dog training** — new dog owners. Big niche.
- **C. Home espresso on a budget** — people buying their first espresso machine and grinder. Big
  niche over time (gear, beans, techniques, repairs).

## Topic test (2 hours each, one keyword tool, results pages read)
- A: 46 easy topics; many questions answered only in forum threads ("why are my shrimp turning
  white", "shrimp molting problems"); typical term 50–300 monthly searches, difficulty 5–25.
- B: 12 easy topics; first pages full of large pet brands and big publishers. Skip rule triggered.
- C: 38 easy topics in comparisons of specific budget models and fixes; head terms hard.

## Scores

| | A. Shrimp | B. Dog training | C. Budget espresso |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Search volume | 3 — real but small; microniche | 5 — huge demand | 4 — steady, growing |
| 2. Competition | 4 — 46 easy topics | 2 — 12 after 2 hours | 4 — 38 easy topics |
| 3. Monetization | 3 — cheap livestock, some gear affiliates | 5 — owners spend heavily | 4 — gear buyers, good affiliates |
| 4. Ease of content | 4 — keen hobbyist writers | 5 — easy to staff | 4 — enthusiasts plentiful |
| **Total** | **14** | **17** | **16** |
| Red flags | none | saturation | none |
| **Decision** | small test | no-go (competition 2) | **go** |

Note how B has the highest total but fails the competition rule: high spending is worthless when
every angle is covered (Anne Moss's reason for avoiding the dog niche). C goes ahead with its 38
topics as the first content plan, and the research continues to reach 50. A becomes a 25-post
small test because it scores 14.
