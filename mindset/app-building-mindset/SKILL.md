# App-building mindset

Seven habits that stop indie app makers from growing their portfolio, based on patterns observed across hundreds of indie developers. Every point here is a common mistake with a concrete alternative.

Source: Dan Kulkov, "building apps? you need to hear this", 21 Oct 2026 (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FTevkxTWtbY)

## 1. Stop copycatting other indie hackers

**The mistake [00:21]:** Building apps copied from other successful indie hackers because they make money, without bringing anything new. You're building apps you're not excited about and have no knowledge about, just trying to steal someone else's pie.

**Why it fails:**
- They already rank for the keywords
- They already have reviews and customers
- You're not bringing anything new to the table
- **Risk:** People can report your app as fraud and you might lose your whole Apple developer account [00:54]

**The fix [01:03]:** Build apps you are personally excited about. You still need to validate keywords and get ASO juice, but you need a unique angle — something you know about, something you're passionate about, or a better execution.

## 2. Stop chasing MRR as a vanity metric

**The mistake [01:14]:** Celebrating monthly recurring revenue (MRR) without tracking what you actually keep.

**The reality [01:22]:** MRR means nothing if your costs are eating it. The speaker's claim (unverified): "I've seen people with $5K MRR making $500 profit."

**The fix [01:30]:** Track and optimize **monthly profit** — what's left after App Store fees, subscription services, server costs, and tools. That's the number that matters.

## 3. Experiment with pricing

**The mistake [01:43]:** Copying the "$70 lifetime" price everyone uses without testing other models.

**Why it's limiting:** You're leaving money on the table by not testing what your specific audience will pay.

**What to try [01:50]:**
- Localized pricing (different prices per country)
- Lifetime plans at different price points
- Subscription vs lifetime experiments
- Higher prices for niche/pro audiences

**The rule [02:05]:** If you're not experimenting with pricing, you're not serious about revenue.

## 4. Stop procrastinating marketing by shipping features

**The mistake [02:11]:** Building feature after feature that nobody asked for, as an excuse to avoid marketing.

**The pattern:** You say "I'll market it when it's ready" but it's never ready. You keep adding features to feel productive while avoiding the hard work of getting users.

**The truth [02:21]:** Your app doesn't need more features. It needs **users**. And users come from marketing, not features.

**The fix [02:27]:** Ship what you have. Start marketing now. Build features when users actually request them, not preemptively.

## 5. Ship updates when users ask, not before

**The expansion [02:32]:** Users don't care about features they didn't ask for. They care about:
- Bugs being fixed
- The features they specifically requested
- Performance improvements they notice

**The rule:** User-requested feature > Your clever idea for a feature nobody wants.

## 6. Do active marketing, don't just wait for ASO

**The mistake [02:42]:** Believing App Store Optimization is enough. "Just optimize keywords and users will come."

**The reality [02:48]:** ASO is passive. It works, but slowly. Most successful apps combine ASO with **active outreach**:
- Social media posts
- Community engagement
- Email outreach
- Content creation
- Direct user acquisition

**Example [02:56]:** Spending 3 hours making TikTok carousels that get 200 views is wasted time. Those 3 hours could go to proven channels that actually bring users.

**The rule:** If a marketing channel isn't bringing measurable users after a fair test, stop it and try something else.

## 7. Secure your Apple developer account

**The risk [03:14]:** You can lose your entire Apple developer account if Apple flags apps for:
- Copyright violations
- Copycat/fraud reports
- Spam or low-quality apps
- Terms violations

**Prevention [03:22]:**
- Delete low-performing apps that aren't worth the risk
- Remove obvious copycats (yes, even if they make a little money)
- Clean up sketchy elements (unauthorized content, trademark issues)
- Keep your account above board

**The trade-off:** Losing one $200/month copycat is better than losing your whole account with 5+ apps earning $2K/month total.

## Summary

| Bad habit | Better approach |
|---|---|
| Copycat other makers' apps | Build apps you're excited about with unique angles |
| Track MRR | Track monthly profit (after all costs) |
| Never experiment with pricing | Test localized, lifetime, and subscription pricing |
| Build features to procrastinate marketing | Market what exists; build features users request |
| Wait for ASO to work | Combine ASO with active marketing |
| Waste time on low-performing channels | Test, measure, kill what doesn't work |
| Risk account with copycats | Delete risky apps to protect your whole portfolio |

## When to ignore this

- **Copycat rule:** If you're in a genuinely underserved niche (keywords exist, no good apps), building a better version isn't a copycat — it's serving users. The rule targets lazy copies of well-served markets.
- **Pricing experiments:** Early on, simple pricing (one lifetime price) reduces decisions. Experiment once you have traffic.
- **Marketing procrastination:** Some apps genuinely need core features before launch (e.g., a broken camera app). The rule targets endless "just one more thing" before marketing.
