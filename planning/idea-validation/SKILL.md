# Idea validation

An eight-step filter that turns a list of problems into one idea worth building, and proves people will pay before any code is written. Most ideas die at step 4 (desperate). That is the point.

Source: Michia Rohrssen, "$1M startup with AI" (https://youtu.be/IAzwllumUo4)

## 1. Collect seeds, not solutions

- A seed is one sentence: the problem, who has it, and what causes it. No product in it yet.
- Keep one pinned list. Add to it daily from your own work, forums, reviews and complaints.
- Review the whole list every 2 weeks and move the strongest into step 2.

Bad seed: "An AI app for restaurants." Good seed: "Small ramen shops lose tourist customers because their menus and ticket machines are Japanese only."

## 2. Follow the money

- Look for clusters of 3 or more funded companies working on the same industry (YC directory, Crunchbase, TechCrunch).
- A cluster means investors saw a change worth betting on. Ask: what changed? Who loses if these companies win?
- No cluster is not a dead end, but it means you are the one proving the market.

## 3. Vehicle filter

- Write down your real goal first: income target ($10k/month is a different business from $100M), where you live, how much you want to sell by phone.
- Low-touch goals (no sales calls) point to low-ticket products sold through ads, content, SEO or the App Store.
- Score every seed 1 to 10 for fit with that goal. Cut anything under 5, however exciting.

## 4. Large, early, desperate

- **Large:** size it bottom-up. Number of customers x what each pays per year. Skip top-down "1% of a $10B market".
- **Early:** something is breaking and getting worse. Look for a moving number (a new law, a price rising, a deadline, a platform change).
- **Desperate:** current workarounds are not good enough and not fixing it costs real money or pain.
- Desperate kills most ideas. People often like their bad tool enough to keep it. If the current fix is "good enough", stop.

## 5. Know what you are testing

Never ask "would you try it for free?" Everyone says yes. Ask instead:

1. Tell me about the last time this happened.
2. How do you handle it today?
3. Is it getting worse?
4. What does it cost you (time, money, stress)?
5. What would fixing it be worth to you?
6. Could I show you something when it is ready?

## 6. Test side by side

- Run 3 to 5 seeds at the same time, with about 10 real conversations each. Testing one idea at a time makes you fall in love with it.
- **B2B:** around 50 short cold emails a day asking for advice, not selling.
- **B2C:** an irresistible free guide plus small paid ads; email the people who download it and ask for a 15 minute call.
- Kill any seed where fewer than half of the people "have it badly".

## 7. Presell before you build

- One tool, one problem.
- Form a customer advisory board of 5 to 10 people: a discount in exchange for feedback and a letter of intent (or a paid preorder).
- One narrow customer and one funnel. The traffic, the offer and the close all say the same thing to the same person.

## 8. Scale vertically, then horizontally

- Optimise one funnel for one customer until conversions stop improving.
- Then reuse the same funnel with new words for the next equally narrow customer.

## Output format

When using this skill on a list of ideas, return a table with one row per seed:

| Seed | Vehicle fit /10 | Money cluster (names) | Large | Early | Desperate | Verdict |

Verdict is KEEP, HOLD or CUT, with one line of reason. Then list the top 3 KEEP seeds with the first 10 people or places to talk to for each, and the exact step-5 questions adapted to that seed.

## Rules

- Never invent funding rounds, company names or market sizes. Name the source or mark it unverified.
- Score honestly. A low desperate score is a CUT even if everything else is high.
- Distribution counts as part of the idea: if you cannot name where the first 100 customers come from, it is a HOLD.
